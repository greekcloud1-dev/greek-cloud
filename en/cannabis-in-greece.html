The legal picture

Cannabis in Greece: what is legal, what is not, and what sits between

The answer depends entirely on which cannabis you mean. Greece has three separate legal tracks, and people who blur them reach conclusions that are wrong — sometimes dangerously so.

What is here

  1. Three tracks, three sets of rules
  2. The medical route
  3. Recreational use
  4. Industrial hemp and CBD
  5. What this means for a visitor
  6. Sources and how to verify

01

Three tracks you must not confuse

Most confusion around "cannabis in Greece" comes from the word describing three things that Greek law treats entirely separately:

TrackStatusWho it applies to
Prescribed medical cannabisRegulated and legalHolders of a prescription from a Greek-licensed physician
Recreational useCriminal offenceEveryone, without exception
Industrial hemp and CBDSeparately regulated, recently changedBuyers of oils, cosmetics and supplements

The important conclusion: seeing an open CBD shop in Athens does not mean cannabis is legal in Greece. These are different tracks, and only one of them is relevant to someone seeking treatment.

02 · Medical

The only route open to a visitor

Legislation regulating medical cannabis in Greece has existed since 2017. Actual dispensing in pharmacies began in February 2024, after years in which the regulation remained on paper. Oversight sits with the National Organisation for Medicines (EOF), which approves products and issues licences.

The conditions, briefly:

  • A prescription from a Greek-licensed physician is required. There is no substitute.
  • Collection at a licensed pharmacy only — not every pharmacy.
  • The physician sets the product, concentration and quantity, subject to regulation.
  • The prescription can be redeemed for up to 30 days, inside Greece.

For a visitor this means one thing: the route runs through a Greek-licensed physician, remotely before you travel or during your stay in Greece. The full process is in the guide.

03 · Recreational

Recreational use — no grey area here

Possession and use of cannabis outside a prescription remains a criminal offence in Greece. That holds for small quantities, and it holds for visitors.

Nor does holding a valid prescription grant blanket permission. Use is intended for private accommodation — a hotel or an apartment. Use in public space, on a beach, in a bar or in the street is not covered by the prescription. Driving under the influence is enforced like alcohol, and the prescription is not a defence.

Do not rely on "everyone does it" Social tolerance in a particular place is not a change in the law, and a foreign visitor is exactly who pays the price when enforcement tightens. A prescription is not a licence to use anywhere.

04 · Hemp and CBD

The track that changed recently

Industrial hemp — the low-THC variety — is regulated in Greece separately from medical cannabis, within a European framework. This is the world of CBD oils, cosmetics and food supplements.

This area has changed significantly: Greek legislation from 2026 restricted the retail sale of dried hemp flower to consumers, regardless of THC concentration. Other products — oils, cosmetics and supplements — were not included in that restriction.

This change does not affect the medical prescription route, which continues to operate through licensed pharmacies. More on CBD in Greece on the dedicated page.

Why this is flagged Older guides online still describe an open CBD flower market in Greece. Do not plan around them. This is exactly the kind of information that goes stale and gets people into trouble.

05 · Bottom line

What all this means when you land

  • Do not bring cannabis with you. Not from anywhere, and not with a valid licence from home.
  • Do not take cannabis out of Greece. What is bought there stays there.
  • The only legal route is a local prescription from a Greek-licensed physician.
  • Even with a prescription, use is limited to private accommodation, and does not extend to driving.

The flying page covers what happens at customs in both directions, and why a notarised translation does not solve the problem.

06 · Verification

How to check for yourself

This area moves, so it is worth checking an official source close to your travel date rather than relying on a guide — ours or anyone else's.

  • The National Organisation for Medicines (EOF) — the regulator. Publishes the official standard and licence registers.
  • Your own country's consulate in Greece — for consular guidance.
  • The Greek Ministry of Health — for legislation and regulatory updates.
